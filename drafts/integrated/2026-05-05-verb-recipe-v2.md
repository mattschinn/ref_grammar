# Verb-Building Recipe (v2)

A working procedure for fleshing out the conlang's verb lexicon in a way that showcases the grammar's divergence from GA, rather than producing one-to-one translations of GA verbs.

This document supersedes `verb-recipe.md` (v1). The substantive moves of v1 are retained; the structure and selection criteria have been rebuilt to reflect what subsequent analysis (in `verb-paradigm-verdict.md` and `verbal-system-description.md`) has learned about the verbal system.

The major changes from v1:

- The four-cell volition model (vol-pos / non-vol-pos / vol-neg / non-vol-neg) has been replaced by the volitive/modulated paradigm with the modulated side hosting a four-sub-modality inventory (PH, AB, INC, PS). Polarity (positive/negative) is treated as orthogonal to the volitive/modulated contrast and held aside for separate treatment, since the sub-modality work has so far been done on the positive-polarity grid.
- A second selection axis — **modulated-paradigm coverage** — has been added alongside v1's verb-volition-category typology. Verbs are now selected on two independent dimensions, not one.
- A semantic-territory map (Tier 1/2/3) has been added in §4 as a positive recruitment-target list, complementing v1's anti-GA-mapping tactics.
- The stative-handling deferral in v1 §8 is closed. Statives are now treated through the three stative-domain strategies (activity-coercion, stimulus-promotion, causative) described in `verbal-system-description.md` §4.

A note on terminology consistency: this document uses the v2 terminology from `verbal-system-description.md` (volitive/modulated, sub-modalities, etc.). The companion document `verb-terminology.md` still uses the older volitive/involitive labels and the four-cell volition × polarity grid; that document is on the to-revise list. Where this recipe's terminology and `verb-terminology.md`'s terminology disagree, the verbal-system description's terminology is current.

---

## 1. Goal

Build a verb stock that:

- Foregrounds the **verbal system as a whole** — the volitive/modulated paradigm, the four sub-modalities of the modulated side, the stative-domain strategies, and the recognitional-habitual nominal construction — as the language's distinctive expressive tools.
- Does **not** map cleanly onto GA verbs. The conlang's verb territory should be carved differently — sometimes finer, sometimes coarser, sometimes shifted laterally.
- Lets the conlang's grammar do real expressive work that GA does clunkily or not at all.
- Provides a small set of **showcase verbs** for textbook/mini-game illustration, alongside a much larger set of **workhorse verbs** for everyday use.
- Loads the lexicon with verbs that **light up the modulated paradigm** across multiple sub-modalities, not just verbs that produce one shining cell and four muted ones. This is the new positive criterion v2 adds.

## 2. The Two Selection Axes

Each candidate verb sits on two independent dimensions. Both should be considered when deciding whether to recruit a verb and how to flesh it out.

### 2.1 Axis A — verb-volition category

Where the verb sits with respect to the volitive/modulated contrast at the *outer* level. Carried over from v1 §2 with terminology updated.

#### 2.1.1 Polysemy-resolving (small, found set)

GA already lexicalized two semantically related events under one form, and the conlang's volitive/modulated contrast disambiguates which event is meant.

- *pass out*: go to bed (volitive) vs. fall asleep (modulated).
- Likely candidates from GA: *fall*, *catch*, *drop*, *lose*, *take*, *get*, and similar high-frequency Anglo-Saxon polysemes.
- This category is **closed and found, not constructed**. Don't try to manufacture polysemy GA didn't supply.
- Expected size: 15–40 verbs total.
- Role: showcase verbs for the mystery/witness mini-game and pedagogical materials.

#### 2.1.2 Aspectual-shift (medium, intermediate set)

The two paradigm sides aren't different events, but the modulated reading shifts the aspectual profile of the same event — the volitive cell tends to be punctual/agentive/bounded, the modulated tends to be processual/stative/unbounded.

- *remember*: deliberate retrieval (volitive) vs. involuntary slide into a state of remembering (modulated).
- Likely candidates: *notice, realize, see, understand, learn, recognize*, and other cognitive/perceptual verbs.
- Role: secondary showcase. Demonstrates that the verbal system is doing meaningful work even where GA doesn't supply ready-made polysemy.

#### 2.1.3 Attitudinal (large, unbounded set)

The verb's volitive and modulated cells refer to the same event, with the contrast doing evaluative or attitudinal work: same action, different framing of how the subject came to be doing it. This is the workhorse category.

- *chit-chat*: deliberately maintained Sunday routine (volitive) vs. getting roped into one at the store (modulated).
- Most of the verb stock lives here.
- Role: the system's everyday expressive tool — claiming credit, deflecting responsibility, signaling agency or its absence.

### 2.2 Axis B — modulated-paradigm coverage

How richly the verb populates the four sub-modalities (PH, AB, INC, PS) of the modulated paradigm. This is the *inner* level of selection and is independent of Axis A — a polysemy-resolving verb may be either richly covered or thinly covered on the modulated side, and the same is true for aspectual-shift and attitudinal verbs.

A verb's coverage is described as **rich**, **medium**, or **thin** based on how many of the four sub-modalities yield productive, non-coerced readings. The four sub-modalities and what they want from a verb:

#### 2.2.1 PH — pure happenstance

Hosted by HAB+progressive and PAST+progressive cells. Wants verbs where the event can plausibly happen to the subject without their agency. Broadest of the four — almost any verb hosts PH if the event can be construed as befalling the subject. Achievements (fall, find, notice, realize, catch) host PH richly because the moment of the event is naturally non-agentive. Activities (think, wander, hum, doodle) host PH richly when they can drift into existence without intention. Verbs that *only* make sense as deliberate (sign a contract, vote, file taxes) host PH poorly.

#### 2.2.2 AB — auto-benefactive

Hosted by HAB+infinitive (marked) and PAST+infinitive cells. The most selective sub-modality. Wants (a) action-host semantics so the subject can do the thing, (b) plausible benefit to the subject, and (c) effort or self-direction in the doing. Verbs like *get up, get going, get clean, get organized, get fed, get to bed, get out of the rain* host AB extremely well. Verbs that benefit others (give, help, comfort) host AB poorly. Verbs that are bad for the subject (catch a cold, get lost, fall) host AB only under coerced negative-evaluation readings, which is a live question in the verdict.

#### 2.2.3 INC — inchoative / threshold

Hosted by PROGRESSIVE+infinitive (the paradigmatic INC cell). Wants verbs with a natural threshold or onset — events with a "becoming-such-that" build-up phase. Threshold-event verbs (fall asleep, doze off, walk out, leave, give up, break down, melt down, cave) host INC paradigmatically. Verbs with a graded approach to a state (warm up, calm down, tire out, perk up, sober up) also work. Punctual achievements without build-up (find, notice, realize) host INC poorly.

#### 2.2.4 PS — pragmatic-stative / experiential

Hosted by PERFECT+progressive. Wants verbs that produce a durative event whose ongoing-ness is discourse-relevant. Activity verbs work well (read, walk, cook, watch, listen, talk, write, drive). Cognitive activities (think, wonder, worry, dream, daydream) are paradigmatic. Achievements host PS poorly. Telic accomplishments work but only mid-event.

### 2.3 The two axes in combination

Each verb gets a two-coordinate label: an Axis A category (poly-resolving / aspectual-shift / attitudinal) and an Axis B coverage profile (rich / medium / thin, optionally with sub-modality detail like *rich on PH/PS, thin on AB/INC*). The combined profile drives recruitment and building-order priority — see §6.

A note on what the two axes are doing differently. Axis A describes what the *outer* volitive/modulated contrast does for a verb; it's a typology of vol-vs-modulated relationships. Axis B describes how the *inner* modulated paradigm distributes its sub-modalities; it's a typology of modulated-paradigm richness. The two are orthogonal: any Axis A category can carry any Axis B coverage profile.

## 3. The Stative Domain

Statives — verbs of internal state, attitude, condition (*be happy, like, want, need, hurt*) — sit outside the volitive/modulated paradigm proper. The volitive frame is incompatible with them entirely (GA *be happy* has no *go ahead and-* form), and the modulated paradigm is available only in restricted form (statives access PH, INC, and PS but not AB, since AB requires an action-host).

The conlang fills the gap with three constructional strategies, described in `verbal-system-description.md` §4. From the recipe's standpoint, each strategy is a different way of recruiting a GA stative meaning into the conlang lexicon.

### 3.1 Activity-coercion

Recruit an activity verb whose typical correlate of the GA stative meaning carries the load.

- GA *be happy* → conlang activity verbs *smile, laugh, grin*.
- GA *be sad* → conlang *frown, sigh*.
- The GA stative meaning is reconstructed from the activity's typical correlate. The conlang ends up biased toward externalized / observable predicates.

For lexicon-design purposes, this means: when a GA stative meaning is on the recruitment list, look for the GA activity verbs that correlate with it and prioritize *those* for recruitment. The stative itself may not need a conlang lexical entry at all if activity-coercion does the work.

### 3.2 Stimulus-promotion

Recruit a verb that frames the stimulus as the (grammatical) subject doing something to the experiencer (now object).

- GA *I like Maya* → conlang *Maya, they going ahead and charming me*.
- GA *I find this soothing* → conlang *it goes ahead and calls me, a nice warm bath*.

This is not a passive: it is subject/object reversal with the experiencer reanalyzed as a topic-comment object. For lexicon design: GA experiencer-object verbs (*charm, captivate, soothe, bore, annoy, fascinate*) are the natural targets for this strategy. Their conlang counterparts host the volitive frame productively because the stimulus can be construed as an actor.

### 3.3 Causative

Recruit the modulated paradigm's *get X to V* shape with a third-party causer in the *get*-slot.

- GA *I like Maya* → conlang *Maya, they get me to smile all the time*.
- GA *the math professor exhausts me* → conlang *the math professor, they get me to pass out all the time*.

The causative requires an action-host main verb, so it composes with activity-coercion: the speaker still has to pick an externalizable activity correlate of the internal state. The two strategies are deployed together for many GA stative meanings.

### 3.4 What this means for stative-domain recruitment

The recipe's old "defer statives" instruction in v1 §8 is replaced by an active recruitment strategy. For each GA stative meaning the conlang needs to express:

1. Identify the activity verbs that correlate with it (for activity-coercion).
2. Identify the stimulus-frame verbs that can host it (for stimulus-promotion).
3. Decide whether the bare stative — the *rhiiynii*-class lexical stative, accessed through PH/INC/PS in the modulated paradigm — should also have a conlang entry, or whether the strategies cover the territory adequately.

For most GA statives, the answer to (3) will be that the strategies cover it and a bare stative entry is unnecessary. A small number of GA statives will warrant bare stative entries for the irreducibly internal cases (*rhiiynii* "need/want" being the paradigm example).

## 4. Semantic Territories to Prioritize

The convergence of high Axis B coverage (rich modulated paradigm) clusters in specific semantic regions of GA. These regions are the highest-yield recruitment targets if the goal is a richly colored verbal system.

### 4.1 Tier 1 — verbs that should light up across all four sub-modalities

These are the gold of the lexicon. Recruit aggressively from these regions.

- **Self-care and bodily-state activities.** *fall asleep, wake up, doze, rest, settle in, settle down, calm down, perk up, sober up, get up, get going, ease into, lean back, sink down, sit up, get dressed, shower, eat, drink, settle.* Hits AB (self-directed effortful action), PH (can happen without intention), INC (have natural thresholds), PS (often durative and experientially salient).

- **Cognitive activities.** *think, wonder, worry, dream, daydream, brood, ruminate, reflect, ponder, mull.* Hits PS paradigmatically (the I-was-thinking-about-it-when-X reading), PH richly (thoughts arrive uninvited), INC for the ones with onset (started worrying, started wondering), AB by coercion ("I got myself to think about it" — effortful self-direction).

- **Threshold-transition verbs.** *walk out, drift off, slip away, give up, break down, cave, melt down, slip into, ease into, sink down, lean back.* Hits INC paradigmatically, PH richly, PS for the durative ones, AB for the deliberate-self-positioning readings.

### 4.2 Tier 2 — verbs that light up three of the four richly

Strong recruitment targets, but expect one sub-modality to be thin.

- **Daily-life social-texture activities.** *chit-chat, wander, hang out, putter, tinker, fuss, dawdle, browse, mosey, lounge.* Strong on PH and PS. AB is coerced but available. INC weak — these don't have natural thresholds.

- **State-onset cognitive/perceptual verbs.** *realize, notice, recognize, remember, learn, understand, see, hear, feel.* Strong on PH (the moment of noticing is non-agentive), strong on INC (becoming-aware), strong on PS (the durative state of remembering/understanding). AB weak unless the verb has a deliberate effortful reading. These also double as Axis A aspectual-shift verbs, layering inner and outer richness.

### 4.3 Tier 3 — verbs that produce strong PH/PS pairings, less elsewhere

Useful for filling out the lexicon, but not anchors of the modulated paradigm.

- **Durative perceptual/experiential activities.** *watch, listen, read, watch over, look out, keep an eye on, follow along, observe.* Strong on PH and PS. INC and AB weak.

- **Mid-process accomplishments.** *cook, write, work on, sort through, get through, build, repair, clean up.* PS strong mid-event; PH possible; AB and INC mostly weak or coerced.

### 4.4 What to deprioritize

Verbs that produce thin modulated paradigms are still potentially worth recruiting, but they are not the place to invest first if richness is the goal.

- **Pure-deliberate action verbs** (sign, vote, build, pay, file, ratify, declare). Belong in the volitive; produce mostly coerced or marked readings on the modulated side.
- **Other-directed verbs** (give, help, teach, comfort, serve, deliver). AB and INC weak; PH and PS coerced.
- **Pure achievements without onset** (win, lose, hit, arrive, die — though *die* has cultural weight that may justify exception). Host only PH cleanly.
- **Punctual transitive achievements** (catch, find, notice — the polysemy-resolving class). Produce dramatic Axis A contrasts but the modulated cells themselves are thin.

### 4.5 A design-strategy note

The Tier 1 convergence zone clusters heavily in the **self-state / cognitive / threshold-transition** semantic territory. This isn't accidental: it's exactly the territory where a system grammaticalizing non-straightforward agency *should* be at its richest, because that's the territory where straightforward agency is most often qualified.

A consequence: if the lexicon is loaded preferentially with these verbs, the conlang as a whole will have a distinctive *texture* — speech in the language will lean toward introspective, processual, transition-aware framings of events. Whether that fits the cultural/aesthetic profile of the conlang is a deliberate design question. The current default assumption is yes, but the assumption is worth flagging rather than naturalizing.

## 5. How GA History Drives the Lexicon

Carried over from v1 §3 with minor wording updates. The conlang doesn't inherit GA verbs. It inherits **GA verb uses** — specific constructions that survived selection, reanalysis, and replacement.

### 5.1 Selection

A GA verb participates in dozens of constructions. Only some survive into the conlang. Selection is biased by:

- **Frequency** — high-frequency uses survive; rare ones die.
- **Colloquial grammaticalization** — uses already drifting toward grammatical function in colloquial GA are favored.
- **Fit with conlang grammar** — uses that slot naturally into the volitive/modulated paradigm, the recognitional-habitual system, or the stative-domain strategies are favored. Uses that resist these systems may drop out.

### 5.2 Reanalysis

Surviving uses are re-fit into the conlang's grammar:

- GA *take a walk* (light verb + deverbal noun) → conlang habitual nominal (*the walks*, plural-iterated).
- GA's volition-bearing adverbials (*on purpose*, *accidentally*) → conlang's grammaticalized volitive/modulated cells.
- GA's pragmatic shared-knowledge uses of *the* → conlang's intimate-register habitual marker.
- GA *get oneself V-ing* / *get oneself to V* — already partially grammaticalized in colloquial GA — → conlang's modulated paradigm with concord-shape selecting between PH/AB/INC/PS sub-modalities.

### 5.3 Loss with replacement

Where the conlang's phonology collapses two GA verbs:

- One may win and the other die.
- Both may survive but specialize — e.g., one takes the volitive cell, the other the modulated.
- A periphrasis may fill the gap.
- **Homophonous merger from sound change is a productive lexical source.** Two semantically adjacent stems collapsing into one form gives the conlang verbs whose polysemy isn't inherited from GA but is created by the conlang's own diachronic processes. The volitive/modulated contrast or the sub-modality grid then does the disambiguating work that GA did segmentally.

### 5.4 Three semantic shift patterns

- **Narrowing**: a broad GA verb survives in only one of its uses. (*countenance* → just "face.")
- **Specialization driven by grammar**: when the conlang's verbal system forces speakers to commit to a reading, the more frequent cell shifts the verb's perceived "core" meaning over time.
- **Accretion of grammatical material**: high-frequency volitive verbs may have absorbed bits of the LVCs (*go-ahead-and-*, *got-oneself-*) into their stems, producing suppletion in the volitive paradigm and verbs that "feel" inherently volitive or modulated.

## 6. The Habitual Layer

Carried over from v1 §4 with terminology aligned to the recognitional-habitual treatment in `verb-terminology.md`. The deverbal nominal + recognitional *the* + habitual *-s* system reaches into GA's gerund/nominalization territory. A useful diagnostic for each candidate verb:

- Does the GA source have a salient deverbal nominal use? (*the walk, our walks* — yes. *the eating* — partial. *the being* — essentially no.)
- Verbs with strong deverbal use participate richly in the recognitional-habitual register.
- Verbs without are more action-bound and show their personality primarily through the volitive/modulated paradigm.
- Verbs with **both** rich deverbal nominalization and clean volitive/modulated contrasts and rich modulated-paradigm coverage are the highest-value targets — flesh these out first.

The two-dimensional habitual:

- *the V-ing* — pragmatically marked shared-knowledge habitual; "you know the kind."
- *the V-ings* — adds repeated/iterative reading on top.
- The two stack: shared-knowledge type, repeatedly instantiated.

This layer is orthogonal to both Axis A and Axis B and adds a third dimension to the verb's expressive profile.

## 7. The Diagnostic Questions

For any candidate verb, work through these five questions in order. The verbs that yield satisfying answers across all five are the ones to build out first; thin answers signal candidates to defer or treat as edges of the system.

The diagnostic has expanded from v1's four questions to five, with the new question being modulated-paradigm coverage (Axis B).

1. **Which GA constructions did it participate in, and which survived?** List the GA uses. Mark which are high-frequency, which are colloquial, which are formal/learned. Predict which survive selection.

2. **Where does it sit on Axis A — verb-volition category?** Is the volitive vs. modulated contrast resolving GA polysemy (poly-resolving), shifting aspect (aspectual-shift), or doing pure framing (attitudinal)?

3. **Where does it sit on Axis B — modulated-paradigm coverage?** Test the four sub-modalities. Which cells are productive? Which are coerced? Which are blocked? Record a coverage profile (rich / medium / thin), optionally with sub-modality detail.

4. **Does it have a salient deverbal nominal, and if so, what's the recognitional-habitual reading?** Test *the V-ing* and *the V-ings*. Is the shared-knowledge reading natural? Is the iterative reading natural? Is the verb register-flexible (works across intimate/formal) or register-bound?

5. **Are there cognates or competitors in GA that the conlang's phonology has merged or distinguished?** Check for:
   - Phonologically near-homophones in GA that the conlang's sound changes will collapse.
   - Semantically similar GA verbs that the conlang may force into specialization.
   - GA polysemy that the conlang may split across multiple verbs.

The outputs of questions 2 and 3 together give the verb its two-axis profile; the outputs of questions 1, 4, and 5 give the diachronic and morphological context.

## 8. Two-Axis Building Order

Recruitment priority is set by the combination of Axis A and Axis B profiles. A verb that's Tier 1 on Axis B (rich modulated paradigm) and falls into Axis A's poly-resolving or aspectual-shift category is the highest-priority target — it lights up both the outer volitive/modulated contrast and the inner sub-modality grid.

A working priority order, replacing v1 §8:

### Priority 1 — flagship verbs

Verbs that score high on both axes:

- Axis A: poly-resolving or aspectual-shift.
- Axis B: rich (Tier 1).

These are the showcase. They demonstrate the verbal system at full power: the volitive vs. modulated contrast does dramatic event-divergence or aspectual-shift work *and* the modulated paradigm fills out richly across PH/AB/INC/PS. Examples: *fall asleep* (poly-resolving via GA *pass out*, Tier 1 self-care/threshold), *remember* (aspectual-shift, Tier 1 cognitive), *walk out* (poly-resolving or attitudinal, Tier 1 threshold-transition).

These should be the first batch fleshed out.

### Priority 2 — workhorse verbs

Verbs that score high on Axis B but plain on Axis A:

- Axis A: attitudinal.
- Axis B: rich (Tier 1) or medium-rich (Tier 2).

These are the everyday vocabulary that gives the modulated paradigm its expressive weight in normal speech. Most cognitive activities, most self-care verbs, most daily-life social-texture activities sit here. *think, wonder, chit-chat, wander, putter, doze*.

### Priority 3 — secondary showcase

Verbs that score high on Axis A but plain on Axis B:

- Axis A: poly-resolving or aspectual-shift.
- Axis B: medium or thin.

These produce dramatic outer contrasts but populate the inner paradigm thinly. Useful for the mystery/witness mini-game and pedagogical materials specifically because the contrast is sharp and easy to teach. *catch, find, notice* fall here.

### Priority 4 — long-tail vocabulary

Verbs that score plain on both axes:

- Axis A: attitudinal.
- Axis B: medium or thin.

The bulk of the lexicon. Recruit as needed for vocabulary breadth.

### Priority 5 — stative-domain recruitment

Run alongside Priorities 1–4 as needed:

- For each GA stative meaning the conlang needs to express, identify activity-coercion, stimulus-promotion, and causative recruitments per §3.4.
- Bare stative entries (*rhiiynii*-class) only for the irreducibly internal cases.

This priority order replaces v1 §8's deferral of statives. Statives are now actively recruited via the strategies, with bare statives being a small specialized class rather than a deferred problem.

## 9. Avoiding GA-Mapped Lexicon

Carried over from v1 §7 with minor additions. Specific tactics for keeping the conlang's verb territory genuinely divergent:

- **Don't ask "what's the conlang word for X."** Ask "what's the conlang's whole vocabulary for the territory X covers in GA," and expect the answer to involve multiple verbs splitting GA's range differently, plus possibly a stative-domain strategy.

- **Look for GA polysemy to split.** *Run* covers physical running, machines operating, organizations being directed, water flowing, candidates campaigning. The conlang's *run*-cognate may take only one of these, with others recruited to different verbs entirely.

- **Look for GA distinctions to merge.** Where GA distinguishes two verbs the conlang's phonology collapses, the merged form may be enriched by volitive/modulated disambiguation, or one sense may take over and the other die.

- **Identify GA volitional twins.** Pairs like *fall/drop*, *forget/put-out-of-mind*, *sleep/go-to-bed*, *learn/find-out* — does the conlang preserve both, merge them, or recruit them to different paradigm sides of a single conlang verb?

- **Watch for sound-change-driven mergers.** When working through derivations, flag cases where two semantically related GA stems will produce identical conlang forms. These are dictionary entries with multiple GA etyma — record both, with notes on which senses came from which source.

- **Check the deverbal nominal slot.** A verb without a recognitional-habitual paradigm is a verb missing one dimension of its expressive range. Either find a GA construction that supplies one, or note that this verb lives in the volitive/modulated dimensions only.

- **(New for v2) Look for GA stative meanings the strategies can recruit.** When a GA stative seems to need a conlang entry, first ask: which activity-coercion, stimulus-promotion, or causative recruitments cover the same territory? Often the stative entry isn't needed because the strategies do the work.

- **(New for v2) Look for GA verbs in the Tier 1 convergence zone first.** When uncertain where to recruit next, default to a Tier 1 region (self-care, cognitive activity, threshold-transition). These are the verbs that pay back recruitment effort with the highest expressive dividend.

## 10. Polarity — held aside

The verbal-system description's modulated paradigm and sub-modality inventory have been worked out for positive polarity. Negative polarity (VOL.NEG, MOD.NEG / from GA *pass on-* and *not quite-* respectively, per `verb-terminology.md`) is a parallel structure that will need its own analysis.

The current expectation is that the four-sub-modality picture will hold, mutatis mutandis, for the negative polarity grid — but this hasn't been verified, and there may be sub-modalities specific to the negative side that don't surface on the positive (e.g., avertive "almost V-ed" might naturally live in MOD.NEG rather than alongside the positive sub-modalities). Until that work is done, the recipe's diagnostic question 3 (Axis B coverage) is to be applied to the positive-polarity grid only, with negative-polarity behavior recorded as observations rather than systematized.

This is a known incompleteness, not a hidden assumption. When the negative-polarity analysis is done, this section and §2.2 will be updated.

## 11. What to Record per Verb

Beyond the dictionary's standard schema, verb entries should include:

- **Two-axis profile.** Axis A category (poly-resolving / aspectual-shift / attitudinal) and Axis B coverage (rich/medium/thin, with sub-modality detail if useful).
- **GA construction inventory** — which GA uses survived, which died, which were reanalyzed.
- **Modulated-paradigm cell coverage** — for each of the six productive cells (HAB+inf, HAB+prog, PROG+inf, PAST+inf, PAST+prog, PERF+prog), is it productive, coerced, or blocked? What sub-modality does the productive cell host? Example sentences for the productive cells.
- **Volitive paradigm coverage** — for the three volitive cells (HAB, PROG, PAST), is the verb productive, marked, or blocked?
- **Recognitional-habitual paradigm** — whether *the V-ing* and *the V-ings* are licensed, and what the readings are.
- **Stative-domain recruitment** (if applicable) — which activity-coercion / stimulus-promotion / causative recruitments share territory with this verb.
- **Mergers and competitors** — other GA verbs whose territory this verb has absorbed or shares.
- **Register notes** — intimate/formal, frequent/rare, marked uses.

This is more apparatus than current dictionary entries carry, and most of it can be deferred to a Notes section. But the two-axis profile, modulated-paradigm cell coverage, and volitive-paradigm coverage should be in the entry from the start, because they're what justify the verb's place in the lexicon and they're how the verb interacts with the rest of the grammar.
