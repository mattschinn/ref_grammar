
I want to update the dictionary with two more entries that are related. 
béntsıìy. /bɛnts'i:/ Adverb, meaning "later". from GA "eventually", /ɪˈvɛn.t͡ʃə.li/ colloquially pronounced /vɛnt͡ʃ.li/. Lemma in question derives from latter. (1) /v/ fortifies to /b/ in onset position. (2) /l/ is surrounded by palatal environment - both /t͡ʃ/ and /i/ - so dark /l/ elides to /j/ then gets smoothed out. (3) after /t͡ʃ/ triggers palatalization of /l/, it itself depalatizes to /ts/, (4) nasalization of /e/ is blocked by /nt/ cluster. Link to nobêt (below)

nobêt, /no'bɛʔ/, means "in a short while, later". from GA "(i)n a bit". where /ɪ/ in "in" is elided , leaving only /n/ in a liason manner(colloquial GA already has this). /ə/ goes rounding after /n/. /ɪ/ lowers to /ɛ/ after /b/. Glottal stop persists. Means "later, down the line". Link to béntsıìy (see above). 

béntsıìy and nobêt have similar meanings but inherent flavors from GA. The latter is short while the former suggests longer duration. Similarly, their use follows GA use: "I'll call you in a bit" sounds good, but "I'll call you eventually" is less natural and would be apporpriate in a much narrow set of scenarios. Also notice that nobêt is absolute and fixed at the moment of discourse ("I'll call you in a bit" is again good here, but "I called you in a bit" is wrong. "He hung up. Then he called me in a bit" sounds still wierd), while  béntsıìy can play relative ("I called you eventually" is quite natural). Before we make entries, can you help me develop these nuances. 

rhilþ from GA "wolf" /wʊɫf/ - means same thing. The sound changes involved are (1) GA initial /w/ becomes /ɻ/, (2) back vowels /ʌ, ʊ, ə/ undergo fronting and merge with /ɪ/, (3) /f/ becomes unconditionally /θ/. 

The volitional-non-volitional paradigm consists of the GO-AHEAD form and GET-ONESELF form.
There are two negative markers SKIP and NOT.QUITE. They interact with the vol-nonvol axis and also behave syntactically distinct. Consider the following examples:

You know the trailers prior to the movie, 
I go ahead and tune them out. (volitional)
I get myself tuning out. (non-volitional, here lack of control or volition)
I'll go ahead and SKIP(.ON) tuning them out. (vol. neg.; "refuse to skip the trailers, go out of the way to watch the trailers". A bit weird here I must say. feels more natural with other examples). 
I do NOT.QUITE get myself tuning them out. (nonvol. neg.; "I could not pay attention, but I end up watching them anyway". has a 'frustrative' feel to it).
I go ahead and NOT.QUITE tuning them out. (I intentionally watch the trailers but only a little bit) 
I do NOT(QUITE) go ahead and SKIP.ON tuning them out. (???)

Then, there are other uses for each of them. 
On my way to the gym when its NOT.QUITE Thursday (before Thursday).
I'm on my way to the bar NOT.QUITE the gym ("rather", less intentional, more a bit apologetic)
I'm on my way to the bar SKIP the gym ("rather", more intentional, perhaps with mutual exclusivity flavor)
Skip! (don't!)
