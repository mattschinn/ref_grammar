# Ideophones and the Expressive Layer

**Version:** v1.1 (June 2026)
**Status:** Sibling reference document, parallel to `phonology.md`, `orthography.md`, and `verbal-system.md`. The language reference's §3.9 is an orientation stub pointing here. Terminology follows `terminology-registry.md`.

**Abstract.** Ideophones are a productive expressive word class that depicts the manner of an event — paradigmatically a speech act — in vivid, often sound-symbolic form. The class is phonotactically marginal, able to break constraints binding on the core lexicon; verbalizable, its members taking a frame and concord to serve as predicates; and multimodal, its members pairing with conventionalized co-speech gesture. An ideophone is obligatory after the quotative verb when no words are quoted, and supplies onomatopoeia elsewhere. This document is the canonical reference for the class — its inventory, phonotactics, gesture notation, and grammatical interfaces; the quotative construction that hosts it is in `verbal-system.md` §12.

This document describes the expressive layer as it currently stands. It is the youngest of the sibling references, and much of it is **[Provisional]** on a single round of translation data; where a question is open, it is flagged.

Status tags — **[Settled]** committed; **[Provisional]** current best analysis; **[Open]** explicitly undecided — and the reading conventions for examples and glosses are explained in `language_reference.md`, "How to read this document set." The gesture-notation convention (**χ**) specific to this document is defined in §6.

## 1. Overview

An **ideophone** is a vivid, often sound-symbolic word that *depicts* rather than *describes* — it performs the manner of an event instead of naming it. The conlang's ideophones cluster around **speech acts**: where GA would reach for a manner-of-speaking verb (*snap*, *drone*, *mutter*), the conlang pairs the quotative verb *o* (`verbal-system.md` §12) with an ideophone that enacts the manner.

> *Beunheumm rhentt, rhemmemmêm.*
> /—/ `[Placeholder: IPA pending phonology pass]`
> 1SG.VOL say.PRF, IDEO
> χ back-of-hand rhythmic clap
> "I said it — boom, boom, boom."

Three properties set the class apart from the core lexicon and organize this document: it is **phonotactically marginal** (§5), **verbalizable** — a member can take a frame and concord and head its own clause (§4) — and **multimodal**, its members pairing with conventionalized co-speech gesture (§6).

## 2. Forms

The attested inventory is small — one round of translation data — and **[Provisional]**. Each entry pairs a surface form with a sense and, where attested, a conventional gesture (χ, §6). `[Lexicon: all members pending dictionary entries — batch D4-B]`

| Ideophone | GA source | Sense | Gesture (χ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *rhemmemmêm* | *boom boom boom* | bullet-pointed, confident, confrontational argument | back-of-hand rhythmic clap |
| *rekriiâ* | *yakitty-yak* | speaking in dialect; rambling on (the GA exonym *Rickety Yak* is this word) | wrist rotation, "and so on" |
| *bauwouwou* | (onomatopoeic) | speaking in General American | wrist rotation, "and so on" |
| *rhitmmtm* | *nuh-uh* | refusal, negation, disagreement | — |
| *riprprp* | *drip drip drip* | dripping (onomatopoeia) | — |
| *bet bät* | *pitter-patter* | pattering (onomatopoeia) | — |

The first group characterizes **manner of speech**; the onomatopoeia (*riprprp*, *bet bät*) depict **sounds** and enter a clause through the marker *-ðs* (§4). *rhitmmtm* shows the class's phonotactic license at its clearest (§5): its *t*'s surface as glottal stops and its stress falls on a geminate syllabic *m*.

## 3. Function

Ideophones do two related jobs.

**Manner depiction.** With the quotative verb, an ideophone characterizes *how* something was said — confidently and bluntly (*rhemmemmêm*), in dialect (*rekriiâ*), dismissively (*rhitmmtm*). The manner is *shown*, not stated: the ideophone is a small performance, which is why gesture so often rides along (§6).

**Onomatopoeia.** Outside speech, ideophones name sounds directly — *riprprp* for a drip, *bet bät* for a patter. These enter a clause through the marker *-ðs* (§4).

A defining trait is **obligatoriness**: when the quotative verb *o* introduces no quoted words — the bare "say it / blurt it out" use — an ideophone is **required** to fill the manner slot (`verbal-system.md` §12.4). One does not simply "say"; one says *boom-boom-boom*, or *yakitty-yak*, or *nuh-uh*. **[Provisional]**

## 4. Distribution

- **After the quotative verb.** The core position: *o* + ideophone (`verbal-system.md` §12), obligatory when nothing is quoted (§3).
- **As a predicate.** An ideophone can verbalize — take a frame and concord and head its own clause (`verbal-system.md` §4.2.3): *rekriiâ* → *rekriiâkm* "chatting."

> *Rhii riauset rekriiâkm nopiiem.*
> /—/ `[Placeholder: IPA pending phonology pass]`
> 1PL get.oneself.IPFV chat.IDEO-CONCORD evening
> χ wrist rotation, "and so on"
> "We were chatting in the evening."

- **As an onomatopoeia-object.** The marker *-ðs* (< GA *this*) introduces a sound-word into a clause, presenting it as the thing perceived: *rimmase nouśnðs riprprp* "noticing the drip-drip-drip." `[Placeholder: segmentation of *nouśnðs* pending]`
- Whether ideophones can be modified, negated, or take their own arguments beyond these positions is **[Open]** (§10).

## 5. Phonotactics

Ideophones are **exempt from constraints binding on the core lexicon** — the clearest sign that they form a class apart. Attested licenses:

- **Rare or non-phonemes**, and clusters the core lexicon does not permit.
- **Ultimate-syllable stress**, against the language's regular final-stress-with-pitch-residue pattern (`phonology.md` §3.4): the ideophone's accent can sit on a syllable the regular rules would never stress (*rhitmmtm*, on the geminate syllabic *m*).
- **Glottal-stop realizations**: *t* surfaces as /ʔ/ in *rhitmmtm*.
- **Reduplication** and partial reduplication as a productive shape (*rhemmemmêm*, *bauwouwou*, *bet bät*).

These are descriptive observations **[Provisional]**, not yet formal rules; the phonotactic-exemption rule itself is the responsibility of `phonology.md`, where it is flagged for a dedicated session. `[New rule needed: ideophone phonotactic exemption — phonology session]`

## 6. Multimodality and gesture

Many ideophones are **multimodal**: the spoken form is conventionally co-produced with a **gesture**, and the pair is the lexical unit. The gesture is not free illustration — it is conventionalized and shared, part of what the ideophone *is*. *rekriiâ* and *bauwouwou* both take a wrist rotation glossable as "and so on"; *rhemmemmêm* takes a back-of-hand rhythmic clap that beats out the bullet-pointed delivery.

**The χ convention.** Because the document set has no prior apparatus for gesture, this document introduces one. The Greek letter **χ** (for *kheir*, "hand") marks the gesture channel:

- In the **inventory** (§2) and in entries, a member's conventional gesture is given in a **χ** field.
- In **interlinear examples**, the gesture appears on its own line, prefixed **χ**, placed after the gloss line and aligned to the ideophone it accompanies:

> *… rekriiâ.*
> … chat.IDEO
> χ wrist rotation, "and so on"
> "… rambling on."

A χ line is added only where a gesture is conventionally part of the form; its absence means no gesture is lexicalized, not that the utterance is still. **[Provisional — the gesture inventory is barely sampled]**

## 7. Orthography

Ideophone spelling uses conventions for which `orthography.md` is the canonical home:

- *t* is written where a glottal stop is heard (*rhitmmtm*), and stress falls on a geminate syllabic nasal — both following the phonotactic license of §5.
- The onomatopoeia-object marker is written *-ðs* (§4).

The interpunct plays **no** special role here: it has no beat- or rhythm-defining function, only the ordinary syllable-division disambiguation it performs across the lexicon (`orthography.md` §3.5), and a reduplicated form such as *rhemmemmêm* — its beats marked by gemination — carries no such ambiguity. This document records the usage; `orthography.md` sets the rule. `[New rule needed: ideophone orthography — glottal-stop *t*, -ðs — orthography session]`

## 8. Origin

The class is built from GA's own expressive resources: onomatopoeia (*boom boom boom* → *rhemmemmêm*; *drip drip drip* → *riprprp*; *pitter-patter* → *bet bät*), reduplicative dismissives (*yakitty-yak* → *rekriiâ*), and interjections (*nuh-uh* → *rhitmmtm*). That the conlang grammaticalized a *manner-of-speech* system out of these — obligatory after the quotative, verbalizable, gesture-bearing — rather than retaining GA's manner-of-speaking verbs is of a piece with its broader habit of building grammar from colloquial, performative material (cf. the quotative from *go*, `verbal-system.md` §12.5).

> *Analytical note.* Whether the gesture pairings are inherited from GA co-speech gesture or are conlang-internal conventionalizations is **[Open]** — a question for the language's pragmatics, with no data yet either way.

## 9. Glossing

- **IDEO** — the gloss tag for an ideophone, whatever its internal shape. A verbalized ideophone glosses as `stem.IDEO-CONCORD` (*rekriiâkm* = `chat.IDEO-CONCORD`).
- **χ** — the gesture channel (§6): a line prefixed χ, after the gloss, describing the co-speech gesture in prose.
- Onomatopoeia introduced by *-ðs* are glossed with the marker segmented where possible.

The internal morphology of an ideophone is generally **not** segmented in the gloss — the form is treated as an unanalyzed depiction — unless a recurring sub-part (a reduplicant, the *-ðs* marker) earns it.

## 10. Open questions

- **Productivity.** Is the class open — can speakers coin new ideophones on the fly — or is it a closed, listed set? The GA-expressive sources suggest openness, but there is no coinage data. **[Open]**
- **The gesture inventory.** Barely sampled; the χ pairings need systematic elicitation, including whether a gesture can occur without its spoken partner. **[Open]**
- **Modification and syntax.** Whether ideophones can be negated, intensified, or take arguments beyond the quotative and predicate positions (§4). **[Open]**
