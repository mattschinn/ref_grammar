Rickett Yack · audio pipeline

From one take to a table of measurements

Two things go in: the generated session sheet, and a single continuous recording. Three stages run over them. Nothing between the microphone and the numbers requires listening to the audio.

Why stage 2 can find a boundary when a silence detector can’t

The original design assumed a pause hierarchy — a short gap after the slate, a long one between items — so a splitter could tell them apart. Measured, the two gaps differed by 0.02 s. Rickett Yack makes it worse: /ʔ/, geminates, /h/ and /sː/ put silences inside words that are as long as the ones between them.

So the anchors carry the structure instead. The rule below is not a guess about where a word might be; it is a consequence of the recording’s measured shape.

What each stage hands on

stagereadsdoeswrites
audio-slate-align.py the take, the session sheet Whisper over 25 s windows; keeps digit tokens only; monotonic LCS against the known order, so hallucinations and repeat-loops drop out instead of shifting every later label anchors.csv
audio-split.py the take, anchors.csv, the sheet clusters the whole take into utterances, assigns slate + word per item, then re-cuts each endpoint at a threshold relative to that token’s own peak segments.csv, one WAV per token
audio-contour.py the take, segments.csv autocorrelation F0 with octave repair, intensity, time-normalised contours, per-item aggregates, pairs compared within block tokens.csv, contours.json, contrast report

When a session doesn’t go cleanly

Which is always. The four things that actually happened in pilot 01, and what the pipeline does about each:

  • A slate isn’t heard (11 of 125). The item is still identity-known — its position in the reading order fixes it — so it is filled in from the utterances between its neighbours.
  • An anchor resolves to the wrong thing. Block 1’s first item anchored on the phrase “Session one.” A bad anchor is demoted, not dropped, and then recovered by the same interpolation. A stage-1 mistake costs one anchor, never the item.
  • A retake, a break, or commentary sits in an interval. Extra utterances are trimmed to the last take, per the spoken-“scratch” protocol.
  • The result is implausible — a 0.14 s “word”, a 3 s one. Rejected and flagged with a timestamp to listen to. Three of 125. Nothing is guessed.

Running it

python tools/audio-slate-align.py audio/masters/session-01.wav \
    --out audio/work/session-01-anchors.csv          # ~12 min on CPU; background it

python tools/audio-split.py audio/masters/session-01.wav \
    --anchors audio/work/session-01-anchors.csv \
    --out audio/work/session-01-segments.csv --write-wav audio/work/items

python tools/audio-contour.py audio/masters/session-01.wav \
    --segments audio/work/session-01-segments.csv \
    --tokens audio/work/session-01-tokens.csv --out audio/work/contours.json
Two invariants. Slates stay English digits, permanently — slating in Rickett Yack would leave nothing to anchor on. And audio/masters/ is append-only: every stage re-reads the same untouched take, so any of them can be re-run with different parameters without a rebuild.
